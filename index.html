CIVE3871 · Design Studio 3.2 · 2026

Call Lane

The brief

Redevelop an existing office building at 20–24 The Calls, on the Call Lane frontage in central Leeds, for Historic England. Narrow streets, red brick and listed buildings, with the River Aire along the southern edge — and a client whose whole purpose is protecting places like it.

The response

Add beside the building rather than into it. A glass envelope held off the historic brick on slim steel mullions, and an oxidised copper element cantilevering ten metres over the western entrance. The Call Lane frontage is left alone.

Office project

The client, the site and the two elements the scheme is made of, with the concept watercolours that settled the materials.

From this sheet

The proposal seen from the north-west and from across the river, with the site it had to fit into.

Glass facade

The envelope snakes around the building in concave bays. It weatherproofs the exposed river elevation and frames the original brick — including the graffiti, which was kept.

From this sheet

The glass is held clear of the historic facade on slim steel mullions. Each bay is concave, warping back towards the building so the reflections pull the eye through the glass and onto the brick behind it.

Copper office

The new element lifts clear of the ground on a recessed brick base and throws its weight forward over the entrance, bracing left visible.

From this sheet

The oxidised copper reads cool against the warm brick — related, and clearly not the same thing. The asymmetrical gable picks up the neighbouring pitched roofs without copying them.

Structural engineering drawing

Frame profiles on three elevations, connection details and a materials schedule, with the services void fitted inside the beam depth.

Floor plans

Four floors, colour-coded by tenancy. The 75/25 split is temporary by design — the shared core is placed so the two halves can be merged later.

CIVE3870 · Design Studio 3.1 · 2025

Nieuwmarkt

The brief

Develop an apartment complex on a 2,200 m² plot in Nieuwmarkt, Amsterdam, falling a metre north to south with a canal along its southern edge. The residents are elderly people and young families who garden and want a sense of community, thirteen minutes on foot from Amsterdam Centraal.

The response

Make the growing space the organising idea rather than an amenity added at the end. Forty apartments behind a red steel ‘living frame’ that unites the tiered facade with the brick block, with greenhouses on the roof and a planted courtyard at the centre.

Amsterdam housing

Client, site and the greenhouse idea, with the development strip running from massing concepts to the initial proposal.

From this sheet

Glass and steel let the rooftop greenhouses blend into the skyline instead of crowning the building, and brick keeps the whole thing in conversation with its neighbours.

Sections

Three cross sections, the steel frame and an exploded drawing separating levels zero to five — the sheet that has to prove the scheme is bold without being garish.

From this sheet

Cut through in three directions, with the courtyard the sections are organised around.

Floor plans

Six levels and forty apartments — fifteen one-beds, twenty two-beds, and five larger units turned into duplexes across the top two floors.

CIVE2860 · Design Studio 2 · 2024

Kirk Hammerton

The brief

Design a semi-detached house on Plot 4 in Kirk Hammerton, a village in North Yorkshire. The client is a wellbeing coach and yoga instructor who lives alone, is emphatic about being outdoors, and teaches clients at home.

The response

Take the geometry from the client and the materials from the site. A roof modelled on falling water and a timber slat facade that moves like a wave, over squared gritstone lifted from the village church so the house still belongs to the street.

Suburban house

Site, client and the finished house, with a street section testing it at the scale of its neighbours.

From this sheet

The street section is the check that lets the geometry be as unusual as it is: however strange the roof, the house still has to sit at its neighbours’ scale.

Elevation design

How the elevations were arrived at, and how the house stands up: reinforced concrete below, a steel-framed roof clad in wave-profiled aluminium above.

From this sheet

The north elevation, and the section cut through all three floors.

Spatial organisation

Working space kept apart from living space, so the house stays private when clients are in it, with every amenity on the ground floor.
